# Cortex Max

Your brain is already a powerful knowledge graph. Every conversation, meeting, and document you add creates memories, neural snapshots, and connections between them. Your agents already search this graph semantically, traverse connections two levels deep, and pull in related knowledge they weren't directly asked about. The brain finds patterns, tracks emotions, scores significance, and compounds across every campaign you run.

Cortex Max adds a narrative layer on top of all of this.

## What changes when you enable it

When Cortex Max is on, Atlas — your Knowledge agent — reads through everything in your brain and writes organized knowledge pages. He takes hundreds of individual memories, snapshots, and connections, and synthesizes them into structured, readable pages about the themes that matter to your business.

This isn't replacing what the brain already does. Your knowledge graph stays exactly as it is — every memory, every connection, every emotional tag. Cortex Max gives Atlas a workspace to build something new on top of it: a living knowledge library that your agents can read like a reference guide, not just search through.

## How the library stays current

The library builds itself automatically. Every time 10 new pieces of information enter your brain — from conversations, meetings, or uploads — Atlas processes them and updates the relevant pages. If a new theme emerges, he creates a new page. If existing pages need updating, he patches them. Your INDEX and CAPSULE stay current.

## What's in the library

Your library is organized into four types of pages:

**Capsule** — A 150-word summary of everything Atlas knows about you and your business. This is the most concentrated piece of knowledge in your entire system. Every time an agent needs the essence of who you are, this is what they read first.

**Topics** — Pages about the big themes in your business. Your brand voice. Your marketing strategy. Your competitive positioning. Your audience. These are cross-referenced with each other and updated as new information comes in.

**Entities** — Pages about specific things. A particular competitor. A specific product. A campaign you ran. A key person or relationship. Detailed, focused, reference-quality.

**Synthesis** — Pages that connect dots across topics. Campaign learnings that span multiple campaigns. How your strategy evolved over time. Insights that don't live in any single memory — Atlas built them by reasoning across everything.

## How agents use the library

Atlas and support agents read from the library when they work. Instead of only searching through individual memories, they get the capsule (who you are), the library index (what Atlas has organized), and the most relevant pages for the current conversation. All other agents continue to get direct access to your knowledge graph with semantic search and graph expansion — exactly as before.

The result: Atlas answers from compiled, synthesized knowledge. Your other agents answer from the raw graph. Both paths are powerful. Cortex Max makes Atlas's path even stronger.

## Health checks

After every 10 library updates, Atlas steps back and audits the entire library. He checks for gaps (memory clusters that deserve a page but don't have one), stale content, contradictions between pages, orphaned pages with no cross-references, and shallow pages built from too few sources. He fixes what he can and logs what he finds.

## Where to find it

Settings > Brain > Cortex Max. Toggle it on per brain — your personal brain, agent brains, or campaign brains. Each gets its own library.

You can view the library at any time by clicking the Cortex Max button in the Brain visualization. The library viewer shows all pages grouped by type, with full markdown content, version history, source counts, and tags.
