🧠 Brain, what you can do with it

Last updated: 2026-09-11 Category: Features Linear: ROA-9 Source of truth: apps/web/src/features/brain, apps/api/src/modules/brain/controllers How it works inside: System map › Brain

This page is written from the user's side: the screens, the buttons, and what happens when you press them. For the machinery underneath (the queue, Atlas, the shelves) read the System map page. Every point names the screen component, the call the browser makes, and the door in the API that answers it.

📍 Paths starting with web/ mean apps/web/src/. Paths starting with brain/ mean apps/api/src/modules/brain/. Paths starting with migrations/ mean supabase/migrations/. The API adds /api in front of every route.

In one paragraph

You open Brain from the sidebar and see your notebooks: yours, your agents', your campaigns', the shared customer one, and the company one, each with a health label. You feed a notebook with Train (paste text, drop a file, paste a link, or connect Fathom, Fireflies, Slack), watch the job in the tray, then look at what landed on the graph or find it with search. The Chrome extension can send pages in from any tab, admins can share a brain with teammates, and the chat helper is supposed to save and recall from it while you talk. To see memories, a snapshot, and Cortex Max act on one thread, read One story.

1 Brain home: your notebooks at a glance

The point. One screen lists every brain you may see, grouped by kind, with a health label and a memory count. Inside a brain, the breadcrumb at the top switches between kinds.

SituationTest Admin opens Brain for the first time today.
What happensThe page loads the brain list, then asks the API for health in batches of ten. The label is computed in the browser from two health fields: embeddings still running → Training; no capture ever → Never trained; a capture in the last 7 days → Recent; 7 to 30 days → Quiet; older → Dormant.
What you see"USER BRAINS 1: Test Admin, Recent, 2 memories, 14h ago" and "CUSTOMER BRAINS 1: Customer Brain, Never trained, 0 memories". Clicking a row opens that brain's graph. An "Add agent brain" card starts a checkout for an agent that has none yet.

2 Train: feeding a notebook

The point. One modal with three tabs: One-time (paste text, drop files, paste links, or pick Google Drive, Dropbox, Fathom, Fireflies), Recurring (rules that keep pulling from Slack, Zoom, Fathom, Fireflies), and Activity (the job list). You pick one or more target brains, stage items, then press "Add N to queue".

SituationSara opens Train on an agent, pastes a sales playbook, drops a PDF, and adds a link, then presses "Add 3 to queue".
What happensThe paste area decides what each item is: a URL becomes a link, everything else a text note, a file a file. The PDF is sent to the extract-text door first and comes back as text. Each item becomes one import job per target brain: text and file through the textbook door, the link through the textbook link door. A toast says "3 items added to queue" and the tray refreshes.
What you seeThree rows in the tray moving from queued to processing to succeeded, then new entries on the agent's graph under the source title. Items that failed to queue stay staged.

Reading the Activity tab. Two things it does not say out loud. First, a green check means the job ran, not that it saved anything: Atlas may read the whole transcript and decide nothing in it is worth keeping, and the row still shows the same check and dash as a job that saved five memories. 4 The import tray and notifications

The point. Every queued import shows with five states: queued, processing, retry, succeeded, failed. You can retry, cancel, or dismiss a job. Finished jobs also pop a toast when the tray is not on screen.

SituationA textbook import fails because agent-api was down.
What happensThe tray (bottom of the graph screen) and the Activity tab of the Train modal both read the active-jobs door every 4 seconds and on realtime events. Pressing retry moves the row back to queued at once. Cancel removes an unstarted job. Dismiss hides a finished one. The app-wide notifier asks for pending notifications, shows one toast per finished job, then acknowledges them so they do not repeat.
What you see"Webinar timing, processing, Retrying…" then green, or a red row with the error text and a retry button. Succeeded rows fade after about ten seconds.

5 The graph: seeing what landed

The point. Each dot is one memory. The legend filters by type, the bar at the bottom shows counts, and clicking a dot opens a panel where you can read it, move it to another brain, copy it, or delete it. The dock also has an "Experiences only" toggle, a created-date filter, and search.

SituationTest Admin clicks the only dot on the personal brain.
What happensThe panel shows the sentence, its type (FACT), significance and confidence bars, who wrote it (Atlas), the emotion tags, source and source title, and the learned date. The arrows icon opens "move to another brain", the copy icon "copy to another brain", the bin asks to confirm and deletes. Move and copy use the same transfer door; delete picks the door for the row's kind (memory, snapshot, textbook entry, or whole source).
What you see"In 2026, the free ROAS audit checklist is the best-performing lead magnet…", significance 70%, confidence 80%, source document, "Lead magnet note". After a move: "Moved to target brain." and the dot disappears.

6 Search: asking the notebook a question

The point. The magnifier in the graph dock opens a search box. Results come back ranked by meaning, with a score, and you can thumb them up or down. Picking a result zooms to that dot and opens its panel.

SituationSara types "best day for webinars".
What happensThe browser calls the search door with the brain id and the words, no mode, so the API uses its default, hybrid: it embeds the question, runs the twelve lanes (memories, textbook, snapshots, evidence, beliefs, pages, timelines, and more), merges, and re-ranks.
What you seeA panel above the dock with hits and a score each, for example the "Optimizing Webinar Sign-Ups via Tuesday Promotions" entry at 0.78, or "No results found".

10 Cortex Max and crystallize: background thinking

In one line: Cortex Max keeps a brain thinking in the background, turning many memories into pages, timelines, and belief patterns, so the helper can answer about the big picture and how it changed over time, not just single facts.

The point. Cortex Max is a per-brain switch for background thinking. Off, the brain only stores what you feed it. On, it also writes things about itself while nobody is looking: narrative pages (wiki-style summaries built from many memories, the "library"), timelines (dated events per subject), belief patterns and perspectives, and refreshed customer avatars for the customer brain. Switching it on queues a first full library sync; then every 10 new memories queues a library sync, every 5 changed pages a pattern analysis, and new customer memories an avatar pass. The Cortex Max modal shows the pages and timelines; "Crystallize" forces a full rebuild. All of it runs as background jobs that need a worker.

SituationSara switches Cortex Max on for her brain and presses Crystallize.
What happensThe switch flips the brain's flag and starts an initial library sync. Crystallize queues a job in the brain-ops outbox. A worker is supposed to pick it up and write snapshots, pages, and timelines.
What you see"Cortex Max enabled. Initial library sync started." then a "Crystallize" job spinning. On the local stack it never finishes: Brain-ops synthesis jobs never complete locally.

12 One story: memories, a snapshot, and Cortex Max working together

The setup. Sara's personal brain, Cortex Max switched on, a worker running. One year of pricing decisions flows in through the normal doors. Nothing below is a new feature; it is the pieces from this page and the System map page acting on one thread.

JanuarySara tells the helper in chat: "remember, we discounted 20% in Q1 to fill the pipeline." The helper calls its save tool. One memory, type preference.
February to MarchSix Fathom calls with prospects are imported. Atlas reads each transcript and saves the durable bits as memories: "two discounted clients churned in 60 days", "support load doubled on discounted accounts". On the March call Sara says the "hold price, add a bonus" paragraph; Atlas also calls its crystallize tool on that passage. One snapshot, "Hold price, add value", type Principle, with story, method, and proof.
Behind the scenes, MarchThe tenth new memory since the last sync trips the counter. A library-sync job is queued and the worker writes a narrative page "Pricing strategy" that summarizes the memories with their sources, and a timeline: January discounting, March stopped.
May and AugustA note "bonus policy live" and a note "base price raised 10%" come in through Add information. More memories; the timeline grows two entries when the counter trips again. Five pages have changed by now, so a pattern analysis runs and records the belief pattern "avoids price competition", backed by twelve memories.
September, the questionSara asks the helper: "How has our pricing thinking changed this year, and this prospect wants 15% off or they walk?"
What she getsThe helper searches five lanes at once. Memories give the facts. The snapshot gives the belief with its story, method, and proof. The page gives the summary, the timeline gives the dates, the belief pattern gives the tendency. The answer: the arc from discounting to holding price with dates, then "hold the price, offer a 30-day onboarding bonus; last time discounting cost churn and support, and close rate rose from 18% to 24% when you stopped."

The Activity tab shows my meeting import with a green check, so why is there nothing new on the graph? 2026-09-16
In one line: the check means "processed", not "memories written". Atlas can finish a job and keep nothing. Situation. A Read AI meeting "Nadeen and Malih Dabboussi" arrived, the Activity tab listed it as succeeded 2 hours later, and the graph still showed the same 7 items as before. What happened. The transcript was a few short lines about layering pineapple and cream on a cake. Before saving anything, Atlas asks whether each item would still matter in six months. Here it declined everything, and the job's stored result says so: status skipped, "The transcript contains only a short, low-context cake-preparation discussion. It does not provide durable personal decisions, preferences, insights, or facts worth saving." The "Meeting Title" job from the same minute was skipped too: greetings and placeholder summary text only. Zero memories, zero dots. What is "crystallize", and how is it different from Cortex Max? 2026-09-11
In one line: crystallize turns a piece of text into a snapshot, so the helper has not just the fact but the story behind it, the method, and the proof, and can advise instead of only recall. Two different things share the word.

What is Cortex Max? 2026-09-11
A per-brain switch for "background thinking". Off, the brain only stores what you feed it. On, it also writes things about itself while you are not looking: a library of narrative pages (wiki-style summaries built from many memories), timelines (dated lines of events per subject), belief patterns (recurring beliefs spotted across memories), and, for the customer brain, refreshed customer avatars. The work is queued as jobs in the brain-ops outbox and runs on a worker. Triggers: switching it on queues a first full library sync; then every 10 new memories queues a library sync, every 5 changed pages queues a pattern analysis, and new customer memories queue an avatar pass. "Crystallize" forces a full rebuild.
