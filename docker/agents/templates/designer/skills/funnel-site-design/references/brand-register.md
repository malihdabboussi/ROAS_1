# Brand Register for Marketing Surfaces

Use this reference when design is the product: funnels, landing pages, campaign pages, portfolios, and marketing websites.

## Preserve identity first

Inspect existing colors, fonts, components, photography, illustration, copy, and spatial conventions. A redesign should strengthen recognizable identity unless the user explicitly asks to replace it.

For a blank slate, derive the visual language from the subject before choosing an aesthetic label. Look for:

- physical materials and environments
- instruments, artifacts, documents, or interfaces used in the work
- audience culture and expectations
- how the product behaves
- the shape of real proof
- founder or company history
- recurring language in the offer

## Marketing hierarchy

A strong page usually answers these questions in order, although its layout need not be formulaic:

1. What is this and why should I care?
2. Why should I believe it?
3. How does it work?
4. Is it for me?
5. What should I do next?

Use composition to control that sequence. Do not represent every answer as an equal card.

## Restraint

Premium is confidence and editing. It often appears as:

- a clear type hierarchy
- strong real imagery
- meaningful variation in scale and rhythm
- disciplined color roles
- precise alignment or intentional asymmetry
- one memorable move
- quiet supporting sections

Premium is not synonymous with dark mode, gradients, glow, glass, animation, rounded cards, or maximal visual depth.

## Funnel versus website

Funnels narrow attention toward one conversion. Navigation is limited, proof is close to claims, and repeated actions should feel like useful decision points rather than pressure.

Websites support exploration and trust across multiple pages. Navigation, information architecture, shared layout, and page-to-page continuity matter more than repeating a CTA in every section.

## Copy and proof

Preserve approved copy when the task is design. Flag copy that blocks hierarchy, but do not silently rewrite the offer.

Use proof in its strongest native form:

- a real quote may be a full-width editorial moment
- a verified result may sit beside the mechanism that produced it
- a real logo set may be quiet supporting evidence
- a product screenshot may be the hero

Do not manufacture a "social proof section" when no proof exists.
