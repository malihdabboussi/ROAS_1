# Deliverable Templates

Load this during Step 5 when assembling the final output. Pick one of the three formats based on audience.

All three formats follow the same voice rules. Load `dylans-super-voice`, diagnose the mechanics rather than the presenter, and apply its Human Enforcement layer before saving.

---

## FORMAT 1 — INTERNAL QC / PRE-RERUN MEMO

**Audience**: pod leader, Nate, Dylan, internal use
**Length**: 2-3 pages
**Tone**: operator-to-operator, tight, no preamble

### Structure

**Section 1 — The Headline (1 paragraph)**
The single biggest thing breaking this webinar and the revenue impact of fixing it. Lead with outcome, not diagnosis.

**Section 2 — Numbers Story (short)**
What the performance data says. Drop-off points. Pitch drop-off. Close rate vs. benchmark.

**Section 3 — Chat Forensics Summary**
- Audience quality verdict (ICP match %)
- Top 10 hottest leads (names + quotes)
- Missed buying signals (count + 3 examples)
- Unanswered questions that killed conversion

**Section 4 — Top 3 Fixes (revenue-ranked)**
For each:
- What's broken
- Why it's costing money
- What the fix looks like
- Estimated revenue impact per run when fixed

**Section 5 — The Follow-Up List**
Named, tabled, ready for the client's sales team to run.

**Section 6 — Re-Run Recommendation**
GREEN / YELLOW / RED with specific conditions.

**Section 7 — Next Steps for the Pod**
Who does what, by when. 3-5 action items max.

## FORMAT 2 — CLIENT REVIEW DECK / MEMO (11 SECTIONS)

**Audience**: client (with Nate or pod leader walking them through)
**Length**: 3,000-5,000 words. Tight, scannable, executive-grade. Not comprehensive.
**Tone**: direct, system-referenced, no finger-pointing
**Visual standards**: apply everything in the SKILL.md "Visual & Branding Standards" section. ROAS.co brand colors, co-branded header/footer, 3-up stat row on cover, action cards, color-coded callouts, emoji on section headers.
**Companion file**: ships with the XLSX lead list (see `references/companion-xlsx.md`). Reference it in Section 7 and the closing action cards.

### Structure (this exact order)

**Section 1 — Cover**
ROAS.co wordmark in purple, "Performance Marketing for Coaches & Consultants" tagline, "Prepared for [Client] · [URL]" line. Below that, a 3-up stat row with 6 hero stats total (two rows of three), color-coded by win or concern (green for wins, navy or amber for context). For live-event ticket offers, lead with seat count, not transaction count.

**Section 2 — At a Glance**
Bullets and short paragraphs, NOT prose. Operators scan first.
- 3-5 bullets on what won
- 3-5 bullets on where money was left
- A 3-fixes table (one row per top fix, with what / why / impact)
- The re-run recommendation (GREEN / YELLOW / RED with one-line condition)

**Section 3 — Where Every Person Went**
The funnel waterfall visualization (horizontal bars showing each drop-off stage: registered → showed → stayed past pitch → bought). Below it, two short subsections:
- What worked (1-2 paragraphs or bullets)
- What broke (1-2 paragraphs or bullets, framed as mechanics not blame)

**Section 4 — Who Was In The Room**
The audience data section. Pull from `references/data-tables.md`:
- Geographic distribution (country level)
- US states breakdown with channel attribution
- Regional rollup with cold-vs-list split
- Top-state city breakdown (especially if live event is in that state)
- Cold ads vs list mix (channel attribution table)
- Demographics from Meta ads manager (age + gender)
- Watch time distribution (horizontal bar chart)
- Industry breakdown of identified chatters
Every table carries a purple info callout with the "what this means" reframe.

**Section 5 — How This Webinar Graded**
The 5-lens scorecard with letter grades AT THE TOP, before the detailed breakdown. Client wants the verdict before the case file. After the scorecard, the 13-category table with score, grade, and one-line rationale per row. Total score and letter grade at the bottom.

**Section 6 — Five Findings**
Numbered 1 through 5. Each finding has:
- An emoji and a clear title
- 1-2 paragraphs explaining what happened, with verbatim transcript or chat pulls where relevant
- The reframe (what it means, what to do about it)
Findings should not exceed five. If you have more, rank by revenue impact and fold the rest into the action cards.

**Section 7 — Where The Money Is**
Short paragraph framing the recovery opportunity. Then a teaser table with the top 5-10 phone leads (name, score, ICP signal, recommended action). Closing line: "Full named list of [N] hot leads is in the companion XLSX, Tab 2: Hot Follow-Up List."

**Section 8 — The 3 Upgrades**
Action cards format. Three cards, one per upgrade, ranked by revenue impact. Each card:
- Title (with emoji)
- What it is (1-2 sentences)
- Why it matters (1-2 sentences with revenue framing)
- `OWNER:` (specific role or person)
- `DEADLINE:` (specific date or window)
- `IMPACT:` (one-line quantified outcome)

**Section 9 — The Rewrite**
Pick the single weakest beat of the webinar and rewrite it in full. Not notes, actual speaking copy. Before/after format. Include the slide visual (or describe it) so the rewrite is grounded in the slide it lives on.

**Section 10 — The Plan**
A 4-week table:

| Week | What gets done | Owner | Outcome |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Fix #1 + chat-drop scripts updated | Nate + pod | Top leak closed |
| 2 | Re-test on micro-segment, finalize new slide | Pod | Stage 2 sign-off |
| 3 | Re-run with full ad spend | Media buyer + pod | New benchmark |
| 4 | Measure + decide next | Nate + Dylan | Plan for run 3 |

Below the table, a short "Decision framework" paragraph that names the 1-2 metrics we're watching and the threshold for GREEN / YELLOW / RED on the next run.

**Section 11 — This Week**
Three closing action cards with the same format as Section 8. These are the immediate-week actions, not the 30-day plan. One card should always be "Run the call list" pointing at the companion XLSX, with `OWNER:` (client sales lead), `DEADLINE:` 72 hours, `IMPACT:` highest-leverage revenue recovery this week.

## FORMAT 3 — MULTI-RUN PATTERN REPORT

**Audience**: client (high-stakes review, usually after 2+ runs with mixed results)
**Length**: 12-20 pages with appendices
**Tone**: same as Format 2, with heavier emphasis on the system-level finding

### Structure

**Section 1 — The Pattern**
The single biggest systemic finding, stated in one paragraph, with the cross-run revenue impact.

**Section 2 — Supporting Patterns**
2-4 additional repeating patterns ranked by revenue impact. For each: what repeats, across how many runs, estimated recovery.

**Section 3 — The Root Cause**
What's actually broken underneath the patterns. One of: offer fit, traffic source, positioning, specific mechanic, moderation, follow-up.

**Section 4 — Consolidated Chat Forensics**
Cross-run audience quality, the unified objection list (top 5 objections across all runs by frequency), and the top moments in chat that repeated.

**Section 5 — The Consolidated Follow-Up List**
Master list across all runs, tiered:
- Tier 1 — attended 2+, scored hot, never bought
- Tier 2 — attended 1, hot, had an unhandled objection
- Tier 3 — attended 1, scored warm, logistics questions

**Section 6 — The Fix Plan**
Specific, sequenced, 30-day plan that addresses the top pattern first. What changes, what stays, what gets tested.

**Section 7 — Marketing Angle Rework**
Pulled from the cross-run chat data. Top 10 new hook angles, positioning rework, objection-reversal copy, creative concept sheet, relaunch ad plan.

**Section 8 — Re-Run Recommendation**
GREEN / YELLOW / RED with specific conditions. Include the specific fix that has to land before the next run.

### Appendices
- Appendix A — Single-run audit for Run 1
- Appendix B — Single-run audit for Run 2
- Appendix C — Single-run audit for Run 3 (and so on)
- Appendix D — Consolidated follow-up list (full table)

## VOICE SAMPLES — HOW THE AUDIT SHOULD READ

These samples show the voice and tone the whole deliverable lives in. Coffee conversation, direct, system-referenced, no finger-pointing.

### Good — headline paragraph
> The audit found one leak responsible for most of what's leaving the room. The offer bridge happens 42 minutes in, and across every run we looked at, 18 to 22 percent of attendees leave right there. That's a slide and a transition, not a content problem. Fixing it once lifts the next run without touching the teaching blocks. Estimated recovery on the next run is in the 30 to 50k range based on prior attendance.

### Bad — same content, wrong voice
> There are several key issues we identified, but the biggest problem is unfortunately the offer transition, which is leveraging a weak bridge that we feel you should optimize. Brutal truth — this is costing you money. You're losing attendees at the 42-minute mark because the transition isn't robust enough to hold them.

Forbidden word count in the bad version: leverage, optimize, robust, "brutal truth", "unfortunately", "we feel." Also uses em dashes and finger-points at "you." All of that has to stay out.

### Good — a fix recommendation
> Here's how we always handle the bridge. Two beats, 60 seconds total. First beat acknowledges the question the room is now asking themselves. Second beat names what they're about to see. No tonal shift, no "so let's talk about what I have for you today" energy. The bridge is earned, not bolted on. We'll rewrite that slide together before the next run.

### Bad — same content, wrong voice
> You might consider restructuring the bridge to be more seamless. We think you could transform the transition by empowering the viewer with a more robust framing that harnesses the emotional momentum you've built.

Forbidden word count: seamless, transform, empower, robust, harness, "you might consider," "we think." Generic, hedging, bolts "consider" onto every recommendation.

## FINAL CHECKLIST BEFORE DELIVERING

Run this before sending any audit deliverable:

- [ ] No forbidden words (leverage, seamless, transform, empower, harness, paradigm, robust, optimize, utilize)
- [ ] No em dashes
- [ ] No triplets
- [ ] No "brutal truth" framing
- [ ] No diagnosis of the presenter (only mechanics)
- [ ] Marketing-function reframe applied (we don't flag our own work as broken to the client)
- [ ] Chat moderation reframe applied (when ROAS was moderating, the fix is the slide, not the moderator)
- [ ] Named follow-up list is actually named, not "several hot leads"
- [ ] Companion XLSX is built and referenced in Section 7 + closing action cards
- [ ] Revenue estimates on each of the top 3 fixes
- [ ] Re-run recommendation is GREEN / YELLOW / RED with a specific condition
- [ ] If no chat transcript was available, it's flagged at the top, not hidden
- [ ] Attribution rules applied correctly for any named public figures referenced
- [ ] Voice reads like a coffee conversation, operator to operator
- [ ] ROAS.co brand colors applied (purple, navy, slate, status colors)
- [ ] Co-branded header and footer on every page
- [ ] 3-up stat row on cover (or 6-up for major reports)
- [ ] Funnel waterfall visualization included
- [ ] Watch time distribution chart included
- [ ] All required data tables included (geo, regional, demographics, channel mix, watch time, industry)
- [ ] Each table has a purple "what this means" info callout
- [ ] Letter grades from 5-lens scorecard placed at the top of the findings section
- [ ] Section 1 (At a Glance) is bullets, not prose
- [ ] If offer is a live-event ticket, leading metric is SEATS, not transactions
- [ ] Total length is 3,000-5,000 words (tight, scannable, not comprehensive)
