# Audit Framework — Five Lenses & Thirteen Categories

The full grading rubric. Load this during Step 2A and 2B of the audit workflow.

---

## THE FIVE LENSES

Every webinar is graded through all five. Each lens catches what the others miss. Every category in the 13-category rubric pulls criteria from multiple lenses.

### Lens 1 — ROAS Framework (our own playbook)
- One Big Idea the whole webinar proves
- Reluctant hero framing, not guru posture
- 3 named teaching ideas in ownable language (the 3 Pillars)
- Case studies woven in with specific numbers
- Coffee-conversation tone (no triplets, no em dashes, no forbidden words)
- Offer fit for traffic temperature (cold traffic needs cold-ready offers)
- Named-client attribution handled correctly (direct vs "collab through")
- Pre-launch three-checkpoint gate held: sequence live, close coached, offer reviewed
- No income claims unless fully compliant and sourced
- Pitch reveal timed with an earned bridge, not bolted on

### Lens 2 — Russell Brunson / Perfect Webinar
- Hook → Story → Offer structure is visible
- Epiphany Bridge story present (the moment the presenter changed)
- Big Domino identified (the ONE belief that makes everything else fall)
- 3 Secrets framework:
  - Secret 1: The Vehicle (new opportunity or system)
  - Secret 2: Internal Belief break (what they believe about themselves that's wrong)
  - Secret 3: External Belief break (what they believe about the world that's wrong)
- Stack slide built with dollar values
- Price anchor → price reveal logic is clean
- Closes 4+ times with variation, not one and done
- Scarcity and urgency are real and stated

### Lens 3 — Dan Kennedy / Direct Response
- Message-Market-Match is tight (who this is FOR is crystal clear)
- USP articulated (why THIS, why YOU, why NOW)
- Irresistible offer (would a rational skeptic say yes?)
- Deadline or reason to act now that is real
- Proof stacked heavy (testimonials, results, third-party validation)
- Risk reversal strong enough to remove the "what if it doesn't work" objection
- Problem → Agitation → Solution arc is used
- Emotional trigger and logical justification both present
- Follow-up sequence planned and visible
- Every element has one job (power of one: one reader, one idea, one promise)

### Lens 4 — Classic Salesmanship
- Rapport established before anything is asked
- Pain surfaced before solution offered
- Trial closes placed throughout, not just at the end
- Micro-commitments built in (small yeses before the big yes)
- Authority positioning earned, not claimed
- Social proof dropped at peak-tension moments
- Objections handled before they're raised
- Assumptive close language used
- Loss aversion activated (what they lose if they don't act)
- Story does the selling, not logic alone

### Lens 5 — Chat Forensics
See `chat-forensics.md` for the full six-part framework (A-F). This is the highest-signal lens. Weight it heavily. A webinar can look tight on script and bomb because the chat was full of the wrong people, or unanswered questions, or silence at the close.

## THE 13-CATEGORY RUBRIC (14 POINTS EACH, ~180 TOTAL)

For each category: score, 1-2 sentence rationale, and flag which lens(es) it failed.

### 1. Pre-Webinar Setup (0-14)
- Opt-in page promises match webinar content
- Confirmation page sets expectation and builds anticipation
- Pre-webinar sequence warms them up (emails + SMS)
- Show-up rate logic (indoctrination content, not just reminders)
- Platform works and doesn't leak attendees

### 2. Audience Qualification (chat-driven) (0-14)
- % of room that matches ICP (pulled from chat intros)
- Decision-maker density
- Industry / stage fit
- Off-ICP contamination rate
- Verdict on whether traffic is the real problem, not the pitch

### 3. Cold Open & Hook (0-14)
- Pattern interrupt in the first 60 seconds
- Stakes established, why THIS next 60 minutes matters
- No "thanks for joining, let me introduce myself" energy
- Curiosity gap opened and held
- Chat signal: did attendees lean in or stay silent

### 4. Promise & Positioning (0-14)
- One big idea, stated clean and early
- Who it's for and who it's NOT for (repels the wrong fit)
- Reluctant hero framing (not guru, not humble-brag)
- Outcome stated in ICP's own language
- Webinar title lives up to what's delivered

### 5. Credibility & Epiphany Bridge (0-14)
- Origin story earned, not performed
- The moment that changed everything is specific and visual
- Credibility woven in, not listed
- Specific to THIS problem, not a general flex
- Chat signal: connection moments land (reactions, "same here" energy)

### 6. The Big Domino / Belief Shift (0-14)
- The ONE belief the whole webinar proves is named
- If they believe this, everything else falls
- Current wrong belief is surfaced and broken
- Makes the old solution feel obsolete
- Chat signal: "mind blown" / "never thought of it that way" reactions

### 7. Teaching Blocks / 3 Secrets (0-14)
- Three named, ownable ideas
- Secret 1: new vehicle / opportunity
- Secret 2: internal belief break
- Secret 3: external belief break
- Each proven, not asserted
- Chat signal: engagement during teaching vs. drop-off

### 8. Story & Proof Integration (0-14)
- Case studies placed at the right beats
- Numbers specific, believable, attributed
- Named clients handled per attribution rules
- Testimonial diversity
- Proof escalates small → medium → big

### 9. Offer Bridge / Transition (0-14)
- Bridge earned, not tonal whiplash
- The question they're now asking themselves is surfaced
- Pitch drop-off stays under 15%
- Chat signal: buying questions start appearing BEFORE the price reveal

### 10. Offer Stack & Pricing (0-14)
- Stack built component-by-component with dollar values
- Price anchor → real price logic is clean
- What it is, who it's for, what's included: zero ambiguity
- Guarantee / risk reversal removes the "what if" objection
- Real urgency with a real reason
- Offer fits traffic temperature

### 11. Chat Moderation & Conversion (0-14)
- Moderator named and active in chat
- Hot leads engaged in real time
- Buying questions answered immediately
- Objections surfaced to presenter
- Chat drops timed correctly (offer link, bonuses, scarcity)
- No buying-signal leakage

### 12. Close, Urgency & Objection Handling (0-14)
- Closes 4+ times with variation
- Top 3 objections (pulled from actual chat) addressed before Q&A
- Chat drops timed and pre-written
- Assumptive language used
- Loss aversion activated
- Final belief-shift moment lands
- Chat signal: urgency language spikes near deadline

### 13. Post-Webinar Flow (0-14)
- Replay sequence live and sequenced properly
- Follow-up emails hit key objections (pulled from actual chat)
- SMS sequence compliant and tight
- Deadline enforced in messaging
- Phone follow-up for the named hot-lead list from chat forensics
- Abandoned cart / incomplete checkout flow built

## SCORING SCALE

- **162-182 (A)** — Ship as-is or keep running. Best-in-class.
- **144-161 (B)** — Ship this week. Minor polish on 1-2 sections.
- **126-143 (C)** — Real rework on 2-3 sections before next run.
- **108-125 (D)** — Major rebuild required. Not launch-ready.
- **Below 108 (F)** — Start over, or the offer itself is the problem.

## HOW TO WEIGHT THE LENSES

When the lens grades disagree, this is the hierarchy:

1. **Chat Forensics** — highest weight. It shows what actually happened in the room.
2. **ROAS Framework** — second. It's our house standard. If we're off-standard, our system is leaking.
3. **Brunson / Kennedy / Salesmanship** — tie. Use them to locate the specific mechanic that broke.

If Chat Forensics grades high but close rate was low, the problem is almost always the offer itself or pitch drop-off, not the audience. If Chat Forensics grades low but the deck looks clean on the other four, the problem is traffic source, not content.
