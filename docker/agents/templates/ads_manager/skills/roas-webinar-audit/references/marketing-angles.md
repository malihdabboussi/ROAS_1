# Marketing Angle & Positioning Extraction

Load this during Step 4 of the audit workflow.

The chat tells you what the market actually cares about, in the market's own words. Every ad, opt-in page, and email that gets written after the audit should pull copy from this section.

This is a working doc the pod leader, copywriter, or media buyer can paste from directly. Keep it specific, keep it in the market's own words, keep it ready to deploy.

---

## THE CORE INSIGHT

Most underperforming webinars have a "build demand" hook when they should have a "recreate what's broken" hook. The "build" framing says "come here, I'll make your current situation better." The "recreate" framing says "your current situation isn't the one you actually want, here's how we rebuild it."

Build framing is weaker because it gives the viewer an easy out: "I'm fine where I'm at." Recreate framing is sharper because it surfaces the pain the viewer is already carrying, then points at a new outcome.

When the chat shows a room that's passive, silent at the close, or full of "I'll think about it" language, the hook is almost always a build hook that should be a recreate hook. This is the #1 pattern in under-performing webinars.

Check the chat for pain the attendees are already describing. If they're writing about situations they want to escape, not just goals they want to hit, the hook needs to shift toward recreate.

---

## WHAT TO EXTRACT (FIVE OUTPUTS)

### 1. New Hook Angles (the pain statements attendees wrote themselves)

Pull every unprompted pain or frustration statement from the chat. Not the things the presenter prompted for, the things attendees volunteered.

Format:
- Direct quote from chat
- Timestamp
- Why it's hook-worthy (what emotion or situation it captures)
- Suggested hook line built from it

Example:
> Chat quote: "I've built the business but I haven't seen my kids play in 2 years"
> Why it's hook-worthy: captures the actual cost of success, the cost that "build more" hooks miss
> Suggested hook line: "You built the business. You also lost 2 years of your kids growing up. Here's how to get them back without losing the business."

Pull at least 10 of these per audit when the chat supports it.

### 2. Positioning Gaps (where build → recreate needs to happen)

Identify every moment in the webinar or the ads where the positioning is "come make it better" and recommend a specific rewrite toward "your current situation is the one that needs to end."

Format:
- Current framing (quote from ad, opt-in, or webinar slide)
- Why it's weak (what easy out it gives the viewer)
- Recreate framing (specific rewrite)

Example:
> Current framing: "Build demand for your coaching business"
> Why it's weak: gives the viewer "I'm fine where I'm at" as an out
> Recreate framing: "Stop chasing warm leads every month. Build the kind of demand that refills your calendar while you sleep."

### 3. Objection-Reversal Copy (the top 3 objections, reframed as ad hooks)

Pull the top 3 objections from Part C of chat forensics. For each one, write an ad hook that pre-handles it in the headline.

Format:
- Objection (quote or paraphrase from chat)
- Ad hook that pre-handles it
- Ideal placement (cold ad, retargeting ad, opt-in page, first 3 minutes of webinar)

Example:
> Objection: "This probably won't work for a service business"
> Ad hook: "Built for service businesses first. If you sell your own time, start here."
> Placement: cold ad headline

### 4. ICP Language Match (phrases right-fit attendees used)

Pull the phrases and exact language the on-ICP attendees used in chat. These go directly into the opt-in page, email sequence, and first 5 minutes of the next webinar.

Format:
- Exact phrase
- Speaker context (stage of business, role)
- Suggested placement

Example:
> Phrase: "I'm stuck at 20k per month and every new client feels harder than the last"
> Context: course creator, 18 months in, mentioned revenue plateau
> Placement: opt-in page subhead, email 1 subject line, webinar slide 3

### 5. Creative Concepts (hook ideas suggested by the chat patterns)

Based on what the chat pattern revealed, suggest 3-5 creative concepts for ads. Each concept should be:

- A one-line concept description
- The angle it leans on (pain, recreate, objection reversal, social proof, authority)
- The format suggestion (talking head, text-only, case-study reel, split-screen before/after, partnership ad)

Example:
> Concept 1: "I built the 7-figure business. Then my wife asked for a divorce." | Angle: recreate, cost-of-success pain | Format: talking-head open, 15-30 sec, native aspect
> Concept 2: "Last month 142 service businesses hit their first 20k month. Here's the one thing they all did." | Angle: social proof + curiosity | Format: case-study reel with name cards
> Concept 3: "If you sell your own time, the 'scale' advice you've been reading is killing you." | Angle: objection reversal (service business fit) | Format: text-only with fast cuts

## PARTNERSHIP AD FLAG

If the client has a partnership ad relationship available (Andy Elliott collab, etc.), flag it here. Partnership ads convert stronger when the audit finds that credibility / authority was the weak lens. Recommend specific partnership ad concepts based on whichever named partner has the right credibility fit.

Attribution reminder when writing these concepts:
- Direct attribution OK: Andy Elliott, Chris Voss, Krista Mashore, Neel Dhingra
- "Collab through our clients" only, never "worked with": Russell Brunson, Dean Graziosi, David Goggins, Gary Vaynerchuk, Dan Martell, Myron Golden, Jeremy Miner

---

## VOLUME AND TESTING GUIDANCE

When the audit recommends new creative, recommend a batch, not one-off ads:

- **Minimum batch**: 15 unique ad concepts
- **Ideal batch**: 50 unique ad concepts
- **Split**: 60% new angles pulled from this audit, 40% variations on what previously worked
- **Format diversity**: at least 4 formats (talking head, case study reel, text-only, split-screen)
- **Partnership ads**: if available, include 5-10 in the batch

This is how ROAS typically runs a post-audit relaunch. Flag in the deliverable so the client knows the audit isn't "write 2 ads and hope" — it's a blitz plan.

### A. Top 10 hook angles (table)
| # | Chat quote (verbatim) | Timestamp | Suggested hook line |
|---|---|---|---|

### B. Positioning rework (3-5 items)
Current framing → why weak → recreate framing

### C. Top 3 objection-reversal hooks
Objection → hook → placement

### D. ICP language bank
10-15 quoted phrases, grouped by where to deploy

### E. Creative concept sheet
3-5 concepts, each with angle + format + partnership flag if applicable

### F. Relaunch ad plan
Batch size, split, format mix, timeline
