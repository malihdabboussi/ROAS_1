# Instagram Carousel — Open-Loop Engine

Turns a concept into a finished carousel whose whole job is to get swiped to the last slide. The mechanic is a **chain of open loops**: every slide closes the loop the previous slide opened, and opens a new one that only resolves on the next slide. The reader is never not mid-loop... that's what pulls the swipe.

Completion rate is the metric that matters. The algorithm rewards carousels where a high share of viewers reach the final slide, so every slide is engineered to make stopping feel like quitting halfway through a story.

## The workflow (always this order)

1. **Research outliers** — pull 3-5 high-performing carousels near the concept, extract what worked. Standing requirement, never skipped.
2. **Lock the hook** — slide 1 carries ~80% of the weight. It opens the first loop.
3. **Build the loop chain** — map every slide as a close + open before writing a word of final copy.
4. **Write the slides** — in the brand voice, short and skimmable.
5. **Close** — soft CTA mid-deck, hard CTA on the last slide.
6. **Caption + output** — caption, loop map, slide-by-slide copy, design direction.

Concept is usually provided. If it's a one-liner, that's fine... the research and loop chain do the heavy lifting. If no concept is given at all, ask for the topic and the audience before researching.

---

## Step 1: Research outliers (standing requirement)

Before writing, find what already worked. Never write a carousel cold.

**Pull 3-5 outliers** near the concept's topic or niche. Search for:
- `[topic] instagram carousel` / `[topic] carousel viral` / `[niche] carousel breakdown`
- Creators known for carousels in the niche (search "[niche] best instagram carousels" or known names... e.g. business/solopreneur outliers like Justin Welsh-style minimalist decks)
- Carousel swipe-file and teardown sites, hook round-ups, "carousels that hit 1M reach" round-ups
- The concept's own brand/competitors... what carousels has this space already posted

**Reality check on access.** Instagram itself is largely gated to scraping. So lean on teardown articles, swipe-file round-ups, screenshots, and creators' own breakdowns rather than trying to fetch live IG posts. If you genuinely can't pull specific examples, fall back to the documented pattern library in this skill and say so... never fabricate fake "viral examples" or invented metrics.

**Extract from each outlier** (this is the point of the research, not just collecting links):
- The hook line + which hook type it is
- Slide count and where the payoff sits
- How it opened/sustained loops (the swipe triggers between slides)
- The CTA and what action it drove (save vs share vs DM)
- One thing worth stealing, one thing to do differently

**Output a 4-6 line research brief** before writing: the patterns that repeat across the outliers, and the specific structural choice this carousel will borrow. Keep it tight.

## Step 2: The hook (slide 1 = ~80% of the weight)

If slide 1 doesn't earn the swipe, nothing else matters. It does three jobs at once:
1. **Stop the scroll** (visual + a sharp line)
2. **Answer "is this for me?"** — the right reader self-identifies instantly
3. **Open the first loop** — tease a payoff that only resolves by swiping

Keep the on-slide hook under ~10 words. The caption can elaborate; the slide cannot.

**Three things a hook headline can do** (pick one, don't blend):
- **Provoke curiosity:** "Are you making these 3 mistakes?"
- **Make a bold promise:** "A week of content in 5 minutes."
- **State a surprising fact / stat:** "This format gets 114% more reach."

**Six psychological switches** a hook can flip (match to concept): curiosity, FOMO, greed (gain), defensiveness ("you're doing X wrong"), relief ("it's not your fault"), disbelief ("this shouldn't work, but..."). Pick the one the audience actually feels about this topic.

**Half-reveal option:** split a line or image across the seam of slide 1 → 2 so the swipe resolves visual tension. Strong when the concept has a clear before/after or reveal.

Always produce **3-5 hook variants** for slide 1 so there's a choice. This mirrors the validate-messaging discipline... different hooks speak to different segments of the same audience.

---

## Step 3: The open-loop chain (the spine)

This is the part most carousels get wrong. They front-load all the value, the loop closes on slide 3, and the reader leaves. The fix: **never fully close a loop without opening the next one in the same slide.**

**The rule per slide:**
- *Close* the loop the previous slide opened (deliver the micro-payoff it promised)
- *Open* a new loop (tease the next payoff)
- The only slide that closes without opening is the final value slide, right before the CTA

**Loop-open devices** (rotate them, don't repeat one):
- The countdown: "Mistake #1..." implies #2, #3 are coming
- The forward tease: "But the third one is what actually moved the needle..."
- The withheld answer: pose the question on the hook, pay it off near the end ("the fix is on the last slide")
- The contrast turn: "That's the easy part. Here's where everyone screws up →"
- The escalation: each slide a bigger claim/stakes than the last
- The "but": deliver value, then undercut it with a new tension ("...except this stops working at scale")

**Anti-pattern:** a slide that wraps everything up cleanly in the middle. If a slide could be the last slide, it's killing your completion rate. Every middle slide must leave something unfinished.

**Worked mini-chain** (concept: "why your webinars stop converting"):
- S1 (hook, opens L1): "Your webinar converted once. Then died. Here's why."
- S2 (closes L1 → opens L2): names the real reason (tracking broke), then... "but that's not even the one that kills most funnels →"
- S3 (closes L2 → opens L3): the bigger reason (no escalation trigger), then teases "there's a point where this becomes unfixable..."
- S4 (closes L3 → opens L4): the deadline/threshold, then "the fix is 3 moves. first one most people skip →"
- S5-7: the 3 moves, each closing the prior and opening the next
- S8 (closes final loop → CTA): the payoff lands, then the ask

Map the whole chain like this *before* writing final copy. The map is the deliverable's backbone.

## Step 4: Structure & length

**Default 8-10 slides.** That's the retention sweet spot. Go 6-7 for a sharp single-idea post, 10-12 for a teardown/case study/guide. Past 12, completion rate falls off a cliff unless it's genuinely serialized storytelling.

**Watch slide 3.** Drop-off spikes around slide 3 (the "is this actually worth it" moment). Make slide 3 a strong loop, not a filler/context slide.

**The arc inside the chain:**
- Slide 1: hook (open L1)
- Slides 2-3: stakes / why this matters now (keep loops live)
- Slides 4-7ish: the value... the meat, one idea per slide, each a loop link
- Slide 7-9: proof / credibility if relevant (result, screenshot, mini-case)
- Final slide: payoff lands + CTA

**Design constraints to hand to the designer (or design tool):**
- 4:5 portrait, 1080x1350px
- Keep all critical text + faces in the upper ~75% (the bottom ~250px gets covered by the caption/UI)
- One idea, one bold key phrase per slide... built for skimmers
- Consistent type + color across all slides (re-orienting every swipe kills flow)
- A visible swipe cue / progress indicator ("1 of 9") lowers abandonment
- Optional: a half-reveal element bridging slide 1→2

This skill writes copy + design *direction*. It does not render the images. For finished creative, hand the per-slide copy and design notes to the design step/tool.

---

## Step 5: The close (CTA)

- **Soft CTA mid-deck** (around the value slides): a low-friction nudge ("save this for your next launch").
- **Hard CTA on the final slide:** one clear action, not three.
- **Make saves rational:** if the goal is saves, the payoff should be reference-worthy... a checklist, script, template, decision tree, set of numbers. People save tools, not vibes.
- **Make shares social:** if the goal is shares/sends, give them the line... "send this to the person who runs your ads."
- **DM trigger (optional):** "comment [WORD] and I'll send it" drives comments → DMs. Only if there's an actual asset to deliver.

One goal per carousel. Decide save vs share vs DM up front... it changes the final slide.

---

## Step 6: Caption

- 60-70 words. Not a re-type of the slides... it adds personality and a deeper line, then points to the action.
- First line is its own hook (it shows above the fold in feed).
- Work in 1-2 natural topic keywords (the feed leans on an interest graph... clear topic signals help distribution).
- End on the same CTA as the final slide.
- Voice: same brand voice as the slides.

---

## Output format

Deliver in this order:

1. **Research brief** (4-6 lines): the outlier patterns + the structural choice being borrowed.
2. **Loop map**: a one-line-per-slide list showing what each slide closes and opens.
3. **Slides**: for each slide... `Slide N` → on-slide copy (verbatim, short) + a one-line design direction.
4. **Hook variants**: 3-5 alternates for slide 1.
5. **Caption**: 60-70 words.
6. **One-line note**: the single goal (save / share / DM) and why.

---

## Voice

The copy is written in the **brand's voice**, not generic carousel-speak. For Dylan's own brands, pair with `dylans-super-voice` (and its anti-AI kill list... carousels are exactly where AI cadence shows: no triplets, no "it's not X it's Y", no staccato drumbeat, no fake-candor badges). For a client, write in that client's established voice.

On-slide copy is shorter and punchier than normal prose, but the same rules apply: say it like a person, cut filler, one idea per slide. Skimmable beats clever.

---

## Final checklist

1. Did I research outliers first and write a brief? → if not, stop and do it
2. Does slide 1 do all three jobs (stop / "for me" / open a loop) in under ~10 words?
3. Is every middle slide a close + open? Could any middle slide be the last slide? → if yes, it's leaking, fix it
4. Are the loop-open devices varied, not the same trick 6 times?
5. Is slide 3 a strong loop, not filler?
6. 8-10 slides unless there's a reason to go longer?
7. One goal (save/share/DM)? Soft CTA mid, hard CTA last?
8. Critical text in the upper 75% safe zone?
9. Caption 60-70 words, own hook first line, topic keyword, matching CTA?
10. Brand voice + anti-AI clean (no triplets, drumbeat, or AI tells)?
