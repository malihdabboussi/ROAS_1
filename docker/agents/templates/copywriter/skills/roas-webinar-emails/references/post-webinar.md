# Post-Webinar Engine — Two Phases: Replay Drive, then Offer FOMO

This is the proven structure (see `assets/swipe-post-webinar.md` for the real flow that doubled bookings). It is ONE machine that runs to the whole list, not two parallel sequences. Show vs no-show is a tone overlay handled at the bottom of this file, not a separate build.

The shape:

- **Phase 1 — Replay Drive (roughly the first 3 days):** every send pushes the replay. Urgency is the replay coming down, and it actually comes down. The last replay day is the takedown day and gets a multi-send stack where the offer gets revealed.
- **Phase 2 — Offer FOMO (the remaining days, usually the weekend):** replay is gone. Every send pushes the offer directly. Urgency is diminishing spots (for a call/application) or a cart-close deadline (for a purchase).

**One email + one SMS per day is the baseline.** The takedown day breaks the baseline with a 3-send stack. If the offer also has its own hard close, that close day gets a stack too. SMS is not optional. It is half the machine. Pair every email with an SMS that carries the same angle in two or three lines.

---

## PHASE 1 — REPLAY DRIVE

### Day 1 (morning after) — the pattern interrupt
Lead with the strongest replay angle available. If the webinar genuinely went deep, use "I revealed too much / I think I messed up" (see `references/angles.md` for the condition, do not use it if the content was thin). Frame the takedown as real and self-protective. Set the deadline and hint it could come even sooner.
- CTA: replay link.
- SMS: the same angle, condensed. "I might have revealed too much yesterday... replay's up but I might pull it early. Watch while you can: [link]"

### Day 2 — "did you watch it" + social proof of action
Light accountability plus the FOMO number: how many people already took action ("5 booked while still on the call, I barely pitched"). Stack 2-3 named case studies. Remind them it might come down sooner than promised.
- CTA: replay link. First soft offer mention goes in the P.S. only ("if you already watched and you're ready to talk, a few spots are open").
- SMS: the action number + watch tonight.

### Day 3 — TAKEDOWN DAY STACK (3 sends) — reveal and push the offer
This is the hinge. Everyone who'll watch has watched, so the offer comes forward now while the replay scarcity peaks.
- **Morning** — offer reveal with scarcity. "Replay comes down tonight. Here's what happened after: [action number]. I opened [N] spots. [N-x] left. Here's what happens on the call/what you get." Both links.
- **Early evening** — "pulling it down tonight. Last time I send this link." Heavier loss framing. Replay link, offer in P.S.
- **Night** — "last call." Two or three lines. Replay link for stragglers, offer link for fence-sitters. "This is the last email about this."
- Each of the three is paired with an SMS.
- **Then actually take the replay down.** The page should read "Replay Gone." A deadline you don't honor poisons every future send.
- Build note: the replay page should host the offer CTA directly below the video, so watching drops the viewer onto the booking calendar / checkout.

---

## PHASE 2 — OFFER FOMO (replay is gone)

Now there's one destination. The replay link is dead, so nothing competes with the CTA. Pure offer, pure scarcity, one email + one SMS per day.

### Day 4 — "which one are you?"
After a week of sharing, split the list in the reader's head: some watched and moved on, some booked and are already getting it built. Which are you? Restate what the call/offer is and the down-ticked spot count ("2 spots left, then I'm heads down on the pipeline").
- CTA: offer link only.
- SMS: condensed version with the spot count.

### Day 5 — "one more thing before I move on" / honest final
The last send. Radical honesty removes the salesy energy: most people won't act, no hard feelings, but a few self-selecting people will book and finally get it built. Stack the best case studies. Final number ("1 spot left") and the real cost of waiting ("next round opens after the next training").
- CTA: offer link only.
- SMS: best case studies condensed + final spot count + link.

If the cart window is longer than two days, extend Phase 2 by repeating the "which one are you / honest" logic with fresh proof and a tightening deadline. If the offer has a hard cart close, that close day gets its own 2-3 send stack (morning / hours-left / doors-closing), same as the takedown stack.

---

## CTA destination by offer type

- **Call / application / limited spots** → booking link, driven by the diminishing-spots countdown.
- **Cart / purchase** → checkout link, driven by a cart-close deadline plus any fast-action bonus expiry. Replace "spots left" language with "cart closes [time]" and give the close day its own stack.
- **Live event ticket** → registration/seats link, driven by seats remaining and the event date.

---

## Show vs No-Show — the tone overlay

The engine above is the same for both segments. If the ESP can't split them, run it blended (the proven swipe is mostly blended and works because it leads with replay and reveals the offer naturally). If it CAN split them, adjust tone only:

**Shows (attended):**
- Replay framing is "in case you want to rewatch" and "remember when I showed [X]." They were in the room; reward that.
- They already saw the offer live, so the Day-3 reveal lands as a reminder, not news. You can lean into the offer slightly faster.
- The "did you watch it" angle becomes "want to revisit any part of it."

**No-shows (registered, didn't attend):**
- Replay framing is "you missed it" / "did something come up?" Never guilt them; a judged no-show unsubscribes.
- The offer is genuinely new to them, so getting the replay watched matters more before the offer push. Keep them in Phase 1 logic a beat longer.
- By the takedown stack and Phase 2, treat them like everyone else. The hard close is the same for the whole list.

Keep a light "if you still haven't watched, the replay is [status]" line for the never-watched segment until the replay comes down. After that, the distinction is gone and everyone gets the offer push.
