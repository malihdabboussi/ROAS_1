# Winning Angles — the plays that drive the FOMO, and when each one is allowed

These are the transferable angles behind the proven flows in `assets/`. Each one has a condition. Using an angle when its condition isn't met is how a sequence reads as fake and burns trust. Match the angle to what actually happened.

---

## REPLAY-PHASE ANGLES (days 1 through the takedown)

### "I revealed too much" / "I think I messed up"
The day-after pattern interrupt. The presenter half-apologizes for going too deep, says people told them they over-shared, and frames the replay takedown as self-protection ("if my competitors get this I'm in trouble"). This is what makes the replay feel valuable and the deadline feel real.

**CONDITION — only use when the webinar actually went deep.** If the presenter genuinely revealed the system, the frameworks, the real numbers, this lands because it's true. If the webinar just touched the surface and was mostly a pitch, this angle is nonsense and the audience knows it. Don't fake depth. When the content was thin, drop this angle and lead the replay drive with the outcome/proof instead ("here's the replay where I showed [specific result]").

### "did you watch it yet" + social proof of action
Day-2 follow-up. Light accountability ("did you see what I sent?") plus the number that creates FOMO: how many people already took action ("5 booked calls while still on the training, and I barely pitched"). The proof isn't the case studies here, it's that *other people in your exact position already moved.*

**CONDITION** — needs a real action number. If nobody booked/bought yet, use early-watcher reactions or the strongest case study instead. Never invent the number.

### The replay actually comes down
The deadline is only as strong as your willingness to execute it. The replay link must die when you said it would. After takedown the page should say something like "Replay Gone." A deadline you don't honor trains the list to ignore every future one.

**CONDITION** — always. If you're not willing to take it down, don't promise to. Use a different urgency mechanic (a bonus expiry, a price increase).

### "I might pull it even sooner"
Unpredictability multiplies urgency. "Friday at the latest... maybe sooner" stops people from assuming they have until the last minute.

## TAKEDOWN-DAY STACK (the 3-send day)

The day the replay comes down is the hinge of the whole flow. It gets three sends, and it's where the offer gets revealed/pushed because everyone who was going to watch has now watched.

- **Morning** — the offer reveal with scarcity. "Replay comes down tonight. Here's what happened after: [action number]. I opened [N] spots. [N-x] left." Both links: replay and offer.
- **Early evening** — "pulling it down tonight. This is the last time I send this link." Heavier on the loss.
- **Night (a few hours before)** — "last call." Two or three lines. Replay link for the stragglers, offer link for the fence-sitters. "This is the last email about this."

For a hard-cart offer instead of a call/application, the cart-close day gets this same 3-send stack, anchored to the cart deadline.

## OFFER-PHASE ANGLES (after the replay is gone, the weekend push)

### Diminishing spots countdown
The core FOMO engine for a call/application/limited offer. The number of remaining spots ticks down across the sequence and is stated in every send. Mike's ran 10 offered → 5 booked live → 3 (Fri) → 2 (Sat) → 1 (Sun).

**CONDITION** — the scarcity has to be real or genuinely capped. A countdown that never actually closes is the fastest way to become noise. If the offer is unlimited, switch to a deadline (price increase, bonus expiry, cohort start date) as the urgency instead of spot count.

### "which one are you?"
The Saturday move. After a week of sharing, split the list in the reader's own head: "some people watched and moved on, some people booked and are already getting it built. Which one are you?" Identity-level pressure without insulting anyone.

### "one more thing before I move on" / honest final
The last email. Radical honesty: "most people who read this won't do anything, no hard feelings. But a few people will book and finally get it built." Removes the salesy energy and lets the self-selecting buyer raise their hand. Stack the best case studies here. State the final number ("1 spot left") and the real consequence of waiting ("next round opens after the next training").

### CTA destination depends on the offer
- **Call / application offer** → the booking/scheduling link, with the diminishing-spots countdown.
- **Cart / purchase offer** → straight to checkout, with a cart-close deadline and any fast-action bonus expiry.
- Either way the offer-phase emails point at ONE destination. The replay is gone; there's no second link competing anymore.

## REACTIVATION ANGLES (front end — driving registration from an aged list)

### "they told me not to do this"
The pattern interrupt that opens a reactivation push. The presenter is doing something their own team thinks is crazy (giving away the $X system for free, one time). Frames the webinar as a rare event.

### "it's not the tech, it's not the time"
Names the reader's stated excuses out loud, then reframes the real problem as a missing system (not a missing skill). Bridges to "that's what the training shows."

### "I'll be honest with you" (the aged-list call-out)
Acknowledges the reader has been on the list a while and never took the next step, without judgment. "You know better than I do why." Then: the people who launched weren't more ready, they just stopped waiting. Works specifically on cold/aged segments.

### "the room is still small / this goes to the cold list tomorrow"
Manufactured-but-true scarcity on registration. The link is currently only going to warm people; it hits the cold list next. Register before it's crowded.

---

## INTENSITY RULE (applies to every angle)

A reminder is not enough. Each send is a packet built to create FOMO and urgency, not a notification. Before any email or SMS ships, it has to carry at least one of: a real deadline counting down, a real scarcity (spots/seats), loss framing (what disappears and when), or proof that other people already moved. If a send has none of those, it's filler. Cut it or rebuild it.
