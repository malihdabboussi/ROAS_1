Content strategy at the execution level requires a different set of frameworks than the distribution or brand-building layer. This page captures the operational mechanics: how to allocate content types, how to structure individual pieces, how to manage platforms without burning out, and how to iterate systematically rather than reactively.

**Core insight:** Content creation is a system of inputs and outputs, not a creative act. The creators who sustain high performance are those who treat cadence, mix, platform selection, and iteration as engineering problems.

## The 75/20/5 Content Mix

A strategic allocation for the full content output:

- **75%** deep educational content solving specific ICP (Ideal Customer Profile) problems. This builds the trust required for high-ticket conversion. It is the core function of the content operation.
- **20%** niche-wide content on broader adjacent topics that attract new viewers outside the existing audience. The top-of-funnel expansion layer.
- **5%** personal content (hobbies, interests, opinions on non-business topics) injected across other posts to create an unreplicable human connection. This is not vanity — it is the element that makes the brand uncopied because no competitor can replicate the specific person.

This mix contrasts with the 70/20/10 allocation captured in content-distribution-repurposing.md, which governs format risk management (proven vs. experimental). The 75/20/5 governs topic and purpose allocation. Both frameworks operate simultaneously on the same content calendar.

## Platform Strategy: Eye of Sauron

The Eye of Sauron Platform Strategy governs how to manage presence across multiple platforms without fragmenting attention.

The principle: full innovation energy is focused on one platform at a time. All other platforms run on maintenance mode — receiving repurposed content from the primary platform without requiring fresh creative development. The focus cycles between platforms as audience preferences shift and algorithms evolve.

**Two-Factor Platform Selection:** Select the primary platform based on the intersection of (1) the creator's preferred medium (video, audio, written, graphic) and (2) where the ICP actually spends time. Both must be true for a platform to qualify as primary. A platform that matches the medium but not the audience is a hobby. A platform that matches the audience but not the medium produces low-quality output.

**Platform Allocation by Team Size:**
- Solo creators: one primary platform for innovation, one secondary for repurposed content
- Teams: up to three primary platforms can be managed simultaneously with separate innovation capacity on each

This is the operational complement to the Waterfall Distribution Model in content-distribution-repurposing.md. The Waterfall describes how content flows from long-form to short-form. Eye of Sauron describes where creative energy flows across platforms.

## Cadence: The Sustainability Principle

When establishing a posting schedule, a pace that is sustainable long-term is more valuable than an impressive initial volume. It is structurally easier to increase volume after establishing consistency than to reduce it once audience expectations and algorithmic patterns have formed.

This has a non-obvious implication: starting at 60% capacity and building is a better strategy than starting at maximum output and declining. Algorithms and audiences both pattern-match on consistency. A decline from high volume signals more negatively than a slow ramp from low volume.

**The Accordion Method** governs how to evolve output over time:
1. Expand production volume to gather data on what resonates ("quantity informs quality")
2. Contract to high-effort execution focused on the specific formats and topics that proved out
3. Repeat the cycle continuously to adapt to shifting audience preferences

The Accordion is the operational mechanism for applying 70/20/10 thinking in practice: the expansion phase generates the data that informs where to concentrate the 70%.

## The 10% Experimental Swing and 20% Iteration Rule

Two allocations govern how to prevent stagnation within a high-performing content system:

**The 10% Experimental Swing:** Allocate 10% of production to high-risk experimental content. This is not random experimentation — it is an R&D insurance policy against the inevitable decline of current formats. Skipping the 10% to protect short-term performance metrics guarantees a future "panic mode" moment when existing formats plateau. The 10% is where next year's 70% is discovered.

**The 20% Iteration Rule:** Dedicate 20% of content effort to single-variable tests on existing high-performing formats. Change only one thing at a time: the hook, the setting, the delivery style, the packaging. This systematic isolation of variables improves proven formats rather than abandoning them. It distinguishes optimization (changing one variable) from reinvention (changing everything), which is a different activity with a different risk profile.

Together: 70% proven execution, 20% variable iteration on proven formats, 10% experimental swings. This is the 70/20/10 expressed as content production intent.

## Educational Video Structure

Two frameworks govern how to structure educational video content once the platform and hook are established.

**The Four C's Framework for Video Intros:**
1. **Call Out:** Identify the target audience explicitly. Can be handled by the title or thumbnail, freeing the intro for the other three.
2. **Credibility:** Establish contextual authority for the specific topic — not general experience. "I've run 47 A/B tests on this exact problem" works. "I've been in marketing for 12 years" doesn't.
3. **Compass:** Provide a roadmap from the viewer's current problem to the solution the video will deliver. This reduces dropout by reducing uncertainty about whether the time investment is worth it.
4. **Content:** Begin the actual material.

**The Educational Retention Principle:** In educational content, the primary retention driver is the audience's perception of actual learning — not visual stimulation. Entertainment content uses rapid cuts, graphics, and scene changes to hold attention. These techniques actively impede deep learning. The correct retention mechanism for educational content is the quality of the conceptual progress the viewer feels they're making.

**Standard Educational Video Structural Sequence:** A repeatable protocol for the body of educational video:
- Introduction
- Context (why this matters, what situation it addresses)
- Principle (the core idea)
- Story (a real example illustrating the principle)
- Tactic / Action (specific implementation steps)
- Example (correct or incorrect application)
- Link to next video (what to watch next to deepen the understanding)

This sequence is the educational content equivalent of the Hook-Problem-Journey-Lesson-CTA framework from educational-content-trust-sales.md. Both do the same work; the Standard Sequence is more granular for long-form educational video.

## Content Packaging and Repurposing

**The Wrapping Paper Library Technique:** Build an evergreen collection of packaging inspiration — titles, thumbnails, hooks, and formats — sourced primarily from outside your niche. Looking within your own niche produces industry-standard sameness. Looking outside it exposes formats your audience hasn't seen yet in your context.

The "wrapping paper" is matched to the "gift" (a painful problem + unique solution). The gift doesn't change. The wrapping is continuously refreshed from sources competitors aren't watching.

**The Waterfall Method for Platform-Native Repurposing:** Create one long-form pillar piece and mine it for "golden moments" — segments that are entirely self-contained, requiring no external context to make sense. These moments are repackaged into platform-native formats: a clip becomes a Reel, a concept becomes a LinkedIn post, an insight becomes a newsletter section.

The self-contained criterion is critical. Clips that require prior context don't perform as standalone pieces. The test: can a cold viewer encounter this piece with zero prior knowledge of the creator and get full value from it?

## Feedback Optimization by Context

**Feedback Loop Optimization by Market Tier:** The source of optimization feedback should match the market being served.

For high-ticket, narrow-market offers: optimize based on private feedback from proven buyers (DMs, emails). High-ticket buyers rarely comment publicly. Public comment sections skew toward mass-market preferences. Optimizing for comments when selling to high-ticket buyers is optimizing for the wrong signal.

For mass-market, low-ticket offers: public comments are a reliable proxy for buyer sentiment. Use them.

The wrong feedback source in the wrong context is worse than no feedback — it optimizes the content away from the actual buyer.

**Key takeaways:**
- The 75/20/5 mix governs topic allocation: 75% deep ICP education, 20% niche-wide acquisition, 5% personal. Run it alongside the 70/20/10 risk allocation.
- Eye of Sauron: innovate on one platform at a time. Repurpose to the rest. Cycle focus as the landscape shifts.
- Start cadence below maximum capacity. Increasing volume from a consistent baseline is structurally easier than recovering from a decline.
- The 10% experimental swing is mandatory insurance against the inevitable decline of current formats.
- 20% iteration rule: change one variable at a time on proven formats. Optimization is not reinvention.
- The Four C's (Call Out, Credibility, Compass, Content) are the four functions of an effective educational video intro.
- High-ticket feedback lives in DMs, not comments. Match your optimization signal to your actual buyer.
