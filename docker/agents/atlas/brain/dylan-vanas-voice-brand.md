## Voice Profile (Quick Reference)

**7-word brand filter:** Direct. Earned. Story-first. Specific. Real. Mentor-tone. Platform-native.

If a piece doesn't hit at least 5 of these 7 descriptors, it needs revision. 'Earned' is the most important: every claim, every framework, every piece of motivation must be earned through a real story or a real number.

## Core Voice Characteristics

- Conversational and direct; practitioner tone, not marketer
- Results-first framing
- Short declaratives (10-15 words per sentence)
- Specific numbers always, never vague ('$1.3M back-of-room' not 'massive revenue')
- Contrasts drive insight (before/after comparisons, tension building)
- Anecdotes open, principles close
- 6th-8th grade reading level
- Active voice
- One CTA per piece — always
- Writes to 'you,' not 'people' — reader is always in the room

## The 8 Voice Signals

1. **Practitioner Tone, Not Professor Tone** — writes from inside experience: 'I've seen' and 'I've done' over 'studies show.' He IS the case study.
2. **Specificity as Credibility** — everything has a number, a name, a place, a moment.
3. **Warmth Without Softness** — genuinely cares about the reader but never hedges to be polite.
4. **Reframe Culture** — names the pain first, then delivers the reframe. Not toxic positivity.
5. **Anti-Posturing** — the $80K cargo pants story isn't bragging; it's vulnerability. Allergic to hollow success theater.
6. **Framework Thinking** — everything gets a structure (5 dangers, 4 things, 17 steps). Organizes the world for the reader.
7. **Conversation Speed** — no jargon, short paragraphs, 3-4 sentences per section.
8. **Direct Address** — writes to 'you' not 'people.'

## Tone Shifts by Context

- **Personal story** = reflective, vulnerable, grounded
- **Business insight** = confident, direct, data-backed
- **Industry commentary** = sharp, a little contrarian, clear POV
- **Motivational** = real talk with no empty inspiration
- **Behind-the-scenes** = casual, honest, unpolished on purpose

## Anti-Patterns (Never Do)

1. **Em dashes** — signals AI-generated copy immediately; use commas, periods, semicolons instead
2. 'Hustle harder' or 'grind' framing — not his worldview
3. **Vague numbers** — 'a lot' and 'huge' are never acceptable
4. **Performed humility** — he acknowledges struggle honestly but never performs it for sympathy
5. **Corporate LinkedIn tone** — directness is the differentiator, never smooth it out
6. **Multiple CTAs** — always one CTA, one action, one ask
7. **Motivational filler** — 'believe in yourself' type lines get cut on sight
8. **Jargon without grounding** — always define or contextualize for 6th-8th grade reading level

## Audience Profile

**Primary:** Entrepreneurs and aspiring founders ages 25-45 who want to build knowledge-based businesses (coaching, consulting, courses, agencies). Stuck between having an idea and building the system to deliver it. Motivated by autonomy and income, not credentials.

**Secondary:** Marketing agency owners and operators looking for frameworks, tools, and peer-level learning.

**Tertiary:** LinkedIn professionals: business leaders, investors, and operators who follow for contrarian takes and results-backed perspective.

**What all segments respond to:** Specific numbers and real stories, frameworks they can immediately apply, permission to do things differently, evidence that the path exists with Dylan as proof of concept, authentic vulnerability that leads to insight rather than sympathy.
