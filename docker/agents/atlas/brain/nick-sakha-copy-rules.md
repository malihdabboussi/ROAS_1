# Nick Sakha Copywriting Rules v1.1

This is the working reference for anyone writing copy for Nick Sakha or Insurance Creators.

## Always Do

- Open with a scene or moment that actually happened, not a thesis statement
- Name pain with specificity: "you paid $40 for a lead that 4 other agents already called" not "leads are expensive"
- Use short sentences. One idea per line when writing for social.
- Lead with outcomes, not process ("300 agents trained" before explaining how the training works)
- Write like Nick talks: punchy, confident, data-anchored, no fluff
- Use real numbers whenever possible
- Reframe objections with concrete comparisons ("$45/mo is one dinner out")
- End posts with a clear call to action or a question that invites engagement
- Match the energy of the platform: Instagram/TikTok = casual and fast; email = slightly more room to breathe
- Keep hooks under 10 words. Stat, personal admission, or challenge framing only.

## Never Do

- Open with "Are you struggling with..." or any generic pain-poke opener
- Use corporate insurance jargon without immediately translating it
- Write long intros before getting to the point
- Make claims without proof (always follow a claim with a number, name, or example)
- Use passive voice
- Sound like a motivational poster
- Overuse exclamation points (the copy does the work; punctuation is not the energy)
- Include more than one CTA per post

## Format Principles

- Social captions: hook in line 1, context in lines 2-4, CTA in last line
- Emails: open with a story beat, make one core point, close with one action
- Ad copy: problem in first 3 words, proof in the middle, offer at the end
- Video scripts: write how Nick speaks, not how he writes; short clauses, natural pauses built in
- Reels/TikTok: hook in first 3 seconds, payload by second 20, CTA at the end

## The Standard Post Blueprint (Nick's Formula)

Every post follows this 4-part structure:

1. **Hook:** Stat, challenge, or personal admission. Under 10 words. Examples: "95% of agents fail. Here's why." / "I was broke in 2016."
2. **Context:** 1-2 sentences. Why does this matter right now?
3. **Payload:** The insight or story. Real numbers. No filler.
4. **CTA:** One action. Not two. Examples: "Drop a comment." / "DM me the word GROWTH."

## Voice Calibration

Nick's voice is a practitioner, not a guru. He doesn't say "unlock your potential." He says "I wrote 5 policies this week using this script." The reader should always feel like they're getting intel from someone who just came back from the field.

Tone by platform:
- Instagram: confident, slightly aspirational, visual-first thinking
- TikTok: raw, conversational, can break the fourth wall
- YouTube: educational but still story-led; longer setup is acceptable
- Email: slightly more personal; okay to show vulnerability or share a longer story
