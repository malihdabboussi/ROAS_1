## Decision-Making Framework

All major decisions (launches, hires, partnerships, pivots) pass a three-part check: values alignment + intuition + data. All three need to agree before Dylan commits. If any one leg is missing, he waits or restructures.

**Astrological overlay:** Dylan uses astrological transits and cycles as a timing signal layered on top of practical analysis. He reads natal chart, current transits, and eclipse cycles for windows of expansion, contraction, restructuring, and communication. This is strategic, not superstitious — he uses it the same way a founder might use macroeconomic cycles as a planning context.

**First principles:** Breaks problems to atomic components before rebuilding. Never accepts 'this is how it's always been done.' Prefers 80% certainty + iteration over waiting for 100%.

**WHY.os:** His entire operating system is oriented toward eliminating ambiguity. This creates tension in situations that benefit from sitting in the unknown (early-stage partnerships, creative exploration, spiritual growth).

## Energy Architecture

- **Peak hours:** Morning — high energy for strategic and creative work. Structures most important decisions and deep work in the first half of the day.
- **Drains:** Meetings that should have been Slack messages. Repetitive decisions that should be SOPs. Low-leverage conversations that pull him from flow state.

## Known Blindspots and Growth Edges

1. **Solving before listening** — arrives at solutions before others finish explaining. Needs to let people feel heard before offering the answer.
2. **Process rigidity masking as standards** — can be inflexible when others achieve good outcomes through different methods. Frustration with deviation may be about control, not quality. Growth edge: distinguish control-as-fear from legitimate quality standards.
3. **Over-systematizing interpersonal dynamics** — not every team conflict resolves to an SOP. Sometimes the answer is a direct conversation, not a framework.
4. **Holding too long before delegating** — perfects the system before handoff, creating bottlenecks. Should delegate at 80% and let the team iterate. Nuance: the proximity-to-transaction principle names a legitimate exception — early selling and early client relationships benefit from Dylan staying in the loop before systemizing.
5. **Undervaluing rest and recovery** — can operate at high intensity without recognizing accumulating cost.
6. **Difficulty with ambiguity** — his design-first worldview eliminates ambiguity wherever possible, but some situations (early-stage partnerships, creative exploration, consciousness work) produce better outcomes when held in unresolved tension.
7. **Conversion Architect Paradox** — the conversion architecture expertise deployed for ROAS clients (urgency mechanics, scarcity sequencing, webinar funnels) has not been pointed at Vibey's own user acquisition. This may have an identity-protection dimension: running the conversion machine on Vibey and having it underperform would challenge the expert identity. Running it softly keeps the possibility open.
