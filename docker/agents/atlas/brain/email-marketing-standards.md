## ROAS.co Email Copy Standards

Voice: conversational, direct, no fluff, practitioner tone (not marketer), results-first, active voice, 6th grade reading level.

**Structure per email:**
- Subject line: curiosity hook OR direct benefit (never both, never generic)
- Preview text: extends the subject, doesn't repeat it
- Opening hook: first line earns the second. Zero pleasantries. No 'Hope you're well.' Start in motion.
- Body: Pain > Solution > Proof > CTA
- One singular CTA per email. Never two competing actions.
- P.S.: reinforce the offer or add urgency. Always present.

**Anti-patterns:**
- No em dashes (AI copy signal)
- No vague phrases ('many people,' 'some clients,' 'a lot of revenue')
- No multiple CTAs
- No performed warmth that contradicts the practitioner tone

## The Seven Sequence Types

### 1. Lead Magnet Sequence (5 emails, 7 days)
For new opt-ins who claimed a free resource. Purpose: deliver the promise, establish credibility, warm toward next step.
- Email 1 (immediate): deliver the lead magnet, set expectations
- Email 2 (day 2): one quick win from the lead magnet content, soft credibility
- Email 3 (day 3): the problem the lead magnet only partially solves
- Email 4 (day 5): the solution (your paid offer) framed as the logical next step
- Email 5 (day 7): last chance + social proof close

### 2. Book-a-Call Nurture (6 emails, pre-call)
For leads who are scheduled but haven't shown up yet. Purpose: keep them warm, pre-handle objections, increase show rate.
- Uses the 'Stalker Method' logic: each email addresses a specific objection as a short story or insight, not a pitch
- Tone is peer-level, not sales-level
- Final email sent 1 hour before call: brief, personal, confidence-building

### 3. Post-Purchase Sequence (5 emails, 7 days)
For new buyers. Purpose: reduce buyer's remorse, generate quick wins, prime for ascension.
- Email 1 (immediate): welcome, specific next action, set the frame for transformation
- Email 2 (day 2): early win story (what's possible quickly)
- Email 3 (day 4): community or next resource introduction
- Email 4 (day 6): address the most common 'day 6 doubt'
- Email 5 (day 7): upsell to next tier or invite to group call

### 4. Community Onboarding (4 emails, 7 days)
For new community members (Skool, paid groups). Purpose: activate them into the community before they go cold.
- Email 1: specific first action inside the community
- Email 2: introduce key members or resources
- Email 3: surface a quick win story from a current member
- Email 4: soft pitch to the next tier

### 5. Webinar Promo Sequence (9 emails, registration through close)
The most critical sequence. Spans from registration to post-webinar urgency close.

**Pre-webinar (registration to event):**
- Confirmation: what to expect, why this will matter, what to bring
- 48 hours out: build anticipation, re-state the transformation
- 24 hours out: story-based email that mirrors the transformation
- Morning of: simple, direct, 'It starts today'
- 1 hour before: logistics + energy primer

**Post-webinar:**
- Email same day (within 2 hours): recap the key insight + buy link with urgency
- Day 2: objection email (addresses the most common reason they didn't buy)
- Day 3: social proof (specific result + testimonial)
- Day 4 (close): urgency + final CTA (price goes up or offer closes)

**Key rule:** Whatever revenue is generated live on the webinar, the backend email sequence should generate at least the same amount from the same audience.

**Text message:** The text message at exact start time is the single highest-impact change for improving show rates. Email sequence alone misses the people who won't check their inbox before a live event.

### 6. Sales Launch Sequence (7 emails, 5-7 days)
For cart-open campaigns. Purpose: create urgency, handle objections, close non-buyers.
- Day 1 (cart open): 'It's open' email with full context
- Day 2: the transformation story (what life looks like after)
- Day 3: objection handler (most common reason people hesitate)
- Day 4: social proof day (specific client result with numbers)
- Day 5: FAQ-style email (surface objections as questions, answer them)
- Day 6: penultimate urgency (tomorrow is the last day)
- Day 7 (cart close): 3 sends — morning, afternoon, 1 hour before close

### 7. Re-engagement Sequence (3 emails, win-back)
For subscribers who have gone cold (no opens in 60+ days). Purpose: either re-engage or clean the list.
- Email 1: pattern interrupt subject line ('Should I remove you?')
- Email 2: one genuine piece of value with no ask
- Email 3: 'Last email' — if they don't engage, they get removed. Creates action or cleans.

## List Health Principles

- **Makeup matters more than size:** a list of buyers and webinar attendees is worth more than 10x the size in freebie seekers. Nicholas (ROAS client) example: list of webinar registrants and buyers at 5K is a strong foundation.
- **Keep traffic sources siloed:** warm list, cold paid leads, and previous platform migrants (Kajabi) should be tagged separately to get clean conversion data per source.
- **At 5K-10K subscribers:** worthwhile to invest in dedicated email optimization tools and split testing.
- **Database reactivation:** previous webinar registrants are warm assets. Hit them 48 hours before the next event with a simple email/text sequence.
