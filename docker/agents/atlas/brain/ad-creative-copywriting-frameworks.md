## Core Philosophy

The hook is the highest-leverage element in any ad. It is the only variable that changes across test variants. Everything else (offer, benefits, CTA, scarcity) holds constant while hooks are tested. Once a hook is validated, it becomes a reusable asset that can be repaired with new back-end content to serve multiple funnels.

Ad copy should be written at a 3rd-6th grade reading level. If the ROAS team finds it complicated to read, it is too complex for a prospect scrolling a feed at 11pm.

## The 17-Step Direct Response Framework

Dylan's structural backbone for all long-form sales pages, VSL scripts, webinar registrations, and direct response landing pages:

1. **Callout** — audience + benefit
2. **Headline** — result without obstacle
3. **Back-up headline** — authority/credibility statement
4. **Six intrigue bullet points** — what they'll discover (not what it is)
5. **Explain the problem** — name the pain precisely
6. **Provide the solution** — bridge from pain to offer
7. **Social proof round 1** — credentials, PR, case studies
8. **Features and benefits** — what's included and why it matters
9. **Social proof round 2** — testimonials, specific statistics
10. **Make the offer** — reveal what they're getting
11. **Add bonuses** — spend 3x more time on bonuses than the core offer
12. **Stack the value** — total up the dollar value before price reveal
13. **Price drop** — anchor high, reveal the actual (lower) price
14. **Scarcity/exclusivity** — limited spots, time-bound, or cohort constraint
15. **Guarantee/risk reversal** — remove the reason to say no
16. **Main CTA** — singular action only, never two competing asks
17. **P.S.** — reinforce urgency, never skip this

## Facebook/Instagram Ad Copy Structure

For short-form paid social ads, the template:

**Hook > Offer/Promise > Benefit Bullets > CTA > Scarcity > Link**

Only the hook changes across variants. The body, benefits, CTA, and scarcity stay constant during testing.

**Two proven hook formulas:**
1. **If-Then Statement:** 'If you're [tired of / sick of / frustrated by X], then [promise Y].'
2. **Stat Hook:** '[Percentage]% of [target audience] [pain point or surprising fact].'

**Video ad targeting via language:** Meta's algorithm now distributes video ads based on what is being said, not just audience targeting. A hook that says 'Hey, if you're a tennis coach...' will be served to tennis coaches. This means video hooks function as both creative AND targeting. Format: 'I'm looking for 3-5 [specific ICP] who want to [specific outcome].' Change the ICP callout to create multiple segment-specific variants from one shoot.

## Native Creative Formats That Outperform Polish

- **iPhone Notes screenshot:** Open Notes, type the ad copy, screenshot it, run as an IG story ad. Works because: intrinsic curiosity (people want to see others' private notes), looks organic, Meta's algorithm favors native-feeling formats. Must use iPhone — Android Notes aesthetic does not achieve the same effect.
- **IG Story text:** Type copy directly in an IG Story, download, upload as ad. Same principle.
- **Proven organic content as ad creative:** Content that already has engagement, DMs, and sales conversations is validated. Run it as an ad with a different CTA. Faster to launch, higher probability of working, scales what's naturally working.

## Recurring Brand Hook (Prop Strategy)

For low-ticket funnels: pick one memorable, tangible prop and use it across every ad variation. Christian Osgood's example: a Starbucks receipt showing $3.05 (exact price of his e-book). Repeat the prop so aggressively that prospects later say 'I saw 50 versions of the ad with that receipt.'

Variations: receipt at every property, key hidden inside a coffee cup, pouring coffee on yourself as a pattern interrupt. The prop creates recognition without creative fatigue because the context changes while the anchor holds constant.

## Ad Copy From Customer Testimonials

The fastest source of high-converting copy: listen to customer testimonials and use their exact words as headlines and hooks. Take verbatim quotes and build the ad around them.

Example: If a customer says 'I was just crossing my fingers and hoping for the best, but now I feel like I can actually build a real team,' the ad becomes: 'Instead of crossing your fingers and hoping for the best when hiring, learn how to build a real team.'

Additional principles:
- Match copy specificity to creative type: specific callouts ('attention plumbing owners') for targeted statics, general copy for testimonial videos
- Use emojis to set tone — text is inherently tone-deaf and the reader projects the mood
- Break big numbers into daily equivalents: '160 meetings in 90 days' becomes '1-2 inbound meetings every single day'

## Niche-Specific Enemy Callouts

For niche audiences, replace generic pain points with specific enemies the ICP recognizes from their daily experience.

For mortgage/real estate: instead of 'tired of leads that ghost you,' name the exact lead sources they hate (Zillow leads, Redfin leads, shared leads) and the exact activities they want to avoid (cold calling, delivering donuts, dancing on TikTok). When you list multiple specific 'what it's NOT' elements and eliminate all common approaches, prospects conclude the offer must be something unique.

## Campaign Crisis Protocol

When a campaign is severely underperforming (e.g., $231 per application when target is $20-30):

1. Cut or reduce budget immediately
2. Don't split test against a clearly broken approach — replace it entirely
3. Strip the opt-in to minimum fields (email required, phone optional)
4. Get new industry-specific creatives
5. Rewrite ad copy from scratch using proven formulas (not AI-generated copy)
6. Send the client a proactive update before they discover the problem
7. Push back their billing date if necessary to show good faith

**The worst outcome:** the client discovers terrible performance before you've acknowledged and addressed it.

## Diagnosis Framework (Meta-Specific)

- Compare CPC vs. cost per link click vs. cost per landing page view to isolate where the funnel breaks
- Low CPC but high cost per link click = ad copy problem (not creative)
- Quality ranking below average + engagement ranking above average = copy problem confirmed
- High CPM = audience saturation or wrong audience; cut immediately
- Frequency above 2.0 = rotate creative or expand audience
- **Meta Andromeda:** the algorithm sequences ads automatically. Never turn off a 'high cost' ad without understanding if it's warming the audience for a lower-cost converting ad. Feed diverse creative and let Andromeda find the sequence.

## Client Communication Around Ad Performance

Every time you touch a client's ad account, send them a message:
1. Lead with good news — cherry-pick 3-5 high-quality leads, screenshot survey responses
2. Connect each lead to the specific ad that generated them
3. Sandwich problems in the middle — what the data showed, what you identified, what you're doing
4. Close with forward momentum — what's coming next, what you need from them

This positions the team as proactive rather than reactive, and clients never feel blindsided when they check their own metrics.
