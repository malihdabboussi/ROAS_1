## Dylan's Sales Philosophy

Discovery-first. The first call is 80% listening, 20% calibrated questions. Positions as strategic partner at the business-outcome level (revenue, profit, scale), not vendor at the tactic level (clicks, CPMs). The diagnosis must precede the prescription.

- Uses Voss-style no-oriented questions to test commitment
- Never chases; if a prospect goes dark, sends one thoughtful follow-up (often a label), then moves on
- Never discounts; if price is too high, the scope changes, not the price
- Walks away from structurally broken engagements rather than inheriting the mess
- Sells the transformation, not the mechanism
- Pricing is value-based, not cost-based — priced against ROI the client will achieve

## Pre-Qualification Architecture

Presenting one specific offer is more effective than letting the prospect choose. Pre-qualify before the pitch call so the closer knows which program to present. Eric Cline's team qualification questions: where are you currently, where are you trying to go, what are you making monthly, what does your dream life look like, and could achieving $15-20K/month change things for you.

By the time the pitch deck appears, the lead is already qualified and the closer is purely pitching the right program.

**Sales dashboard discipline:** Track by lead source (AI setter, phone setter, DM setting, direct booking) showing show rate and close rate for each, and by individual closer showing performance per source. Cross-referencing removes the ability to blame lead quality and creates undeniable accountability.

## Assume-the-Sale Technique

After getting an initial surface-level yes, do not re-confirm commitment (signals desperation). Skip to implementation questions that assume the deal is moving forward.

Examples:
- 'What are we going to do when [stakeholder] doesn't want to spend the kind of money required?'
- 'How are we going to know when we're on track?'

These calibrated questions deepen commitment by getting the prospect to solve future problems with you, which is far stronger buy-in than a verbal yes.

**From live sales experience:** When someone returns after thinking it over, assume the sale. 'Hey, you're back. You want to get one or two?' Skips objection handling, frames the conversation around which option they want. The return itself is the buying signal.

## Handling Burned Prospects

For prospects burned by a previous vendor:
- Offer a 30-day opt-out within a longer contract: 'If you're not happy in 30 days, just tell me and I'll let you out of the agreement'
- Three concerns they consistently raise: trial period flexibility, written deliverables with timeframes, clear communication cadence
- Address all three in one conversation to move from skeptical to near-closed

## Pitch Deck Standards

Sales pitch decks on 1-on-1 closer calls should NOT feel like webinars. 15-20 slides maximum. Simple, real language. The deck is visual support while the closer drives the conversation. The script lives behind the slides, not on them.

Key failure mode: 37 slides felt like a webinar presentation rather than a sales conversation tool. Alternate between screen-sharing and face-to-face.

## Commission Structures for Closers

Tiered model: starts at 18% commission, scales to 20% at a performance threshold, reaches 22% at $100K/month in sales. Incentivizes volume. A single-closer model with tiered percentages is more cost-effective than building a full setter/closer team early.

Long-term hire priority: coachability and fit over immediate skill. Someone who learns the product deeply and becomes recognizable to clients is worth more than a skilled closer who won't stick around.

**Niche alignment:** Hard-closing styles from aggressive niches (wholesale real estate, sales courses) cannot transfer directly to softer niches like parenting coaching. The selling style must match the audience's emotional state.

## The Stalker Method (Post-Booking Warm-Up)

After someone books a call, retarget them with 10 short videos addressing common objections between the booking and the call. Keeps prospects warm, builds trust, and ensures they show up prepared rather than cold. Functions as bottom-of-funnel retargeting specifically designed to improve show rates and close rates on booked appointments.

## Value-First Sales Cycle

1. Genuine catch-up to build rapport before any business discussion
2. Deep discovery questions before offering any solutions
3. Provide genuinely useful strategic advice for free (e.g., a webinar framework resource guide)
4. Demo the platform naturally during the call by using it in real time
5. Set a soft organic timeline, then reconnect about scaling when they have proof of concept

Frame: help as a friend first, prove the organic concept, then scale into a paid engagement. No hard close — value delivery and natural follow-up.
