## The Two-Axis Model

Client retention runs on two axes: **performance** and **communication.** Neither alone is sufficient long-term, but each can compensate for temporary weakness in the other.

- Subpar performance + great communication: clients feel cared for and stay longer while you fix the work
- Great performance + poor communication: results speak for themselves, but clients feel uneasy and are vulnerable to competitor pitches
- Strong on both: clients become referral sources and renewals become automatic

**The internal debate (Jeremiah vs. Nefi):** Jeremiah argued results are primary ('if we make them money, they won't leave'). Nefi argued relationship is primary ('make them your friend, work together against the problem'). Both are right — whichever axis you're weaker on is where to invest.

## Weekly Communication Cadence

Dylan's agency communication structure:

1. **Slack channel** — dedicated account manager for async, same-day communication
2. **Weekly 15-minute check-in call** — review campaign performance, no longer necessary
3. **Monday brief** — previous week's results summary + full weekly performance report
4. **Monthly strategy session** — founder or senior partner, higher-level 'how do we scale this' conversation

**The single strongest retention mechanism:** a weekly recurring call. Without it, communication drops, clients lose confidence, and they churn when a competing offer appears. After every client call, send a post-call recap — what was covered, what the plan is, and the Fathom recording link.

## The Proactive Squeaky Wheel Rule

Either you are the squeaky wheel, or the client is. If you proactively chase clients for approvals, homework, assets, and feedback — they never need to chase you. This reverses the power dynamic.

- Give them action items and follow up on them
- Send updates before they ask
- Ask for deliverables they owe you

Clients hired you because they are too busy to manage their own marketing. They will micromanage if they feel you aren't on top of it. Overwhelming them with proactive communication keeps them occupied and builds trust.

## Ad Account Touch Communication Protocol

Every time you touch a client's ad account, send them a message structured as:

1. **Lead with good news** — cherry-pick 3-5 high-quality leads from the last 24-48 hours, screenshot survey responses, share in the client channel
2. **Connect leads to the ad** — screenshot the specific ad that generated each lead
3. **Sandwich problems in the middle** — what the data showed, what you identified, what you're doing about it
4. **Close with forward momentum** — what's coming next, what you need from them

If you do the work but don't communicate it, the client assumes nothing is happening. They escalate to leadership, creating unnecessary pressure.

## Handling a Restless Client (Crisis Recovery)

When a client is restless about paying and hasn't seen results:

1. Get something tangible and working before the next check-in call
2. Build multiple landing page variations and test different ad approaches aggressively over a weekend if needed
3. Come to the call with real metrics (cheap booked calls, applicant flow) rather than promises
4. Bring a senior team member onto the call for authority and credibility

The combination of tangible results plus leadership presence transforms a potentially hostile retention call into a feel-good progress call. The frame shifts from 'why am I paying you?' to 'how are you closing the leads we're generating?'

## Scope Creep Management Protocol

Clients who expand scope beyond contract terms must be managed proactively. Observed pattern (Sidcor case, May 2026): rejecting pre-approved content last minute, demanding urgent replacements, requesting content bank expansion, adding Stories to scope, requesting unrelated projects.

**Dylan's framework:**
- Push back immediately on out-of-scope requests (non-social projects get redirected to founder)
- Build a content bank as a buffer against last-minute rejections
- When scope expansion comes through a junior team member, have the founder address it directly to reset the power dynamic
- Evaluate whether the client's bandwidth cost exceeds their revenue value

## The Coachability Multiplier

Client coachability is the strongest predictor of service delivery success. Client Josh (May 2026) was expected to underperform ('massive flop') but became one of the biggest wins because he implemented every piece of feedback without hesitation, collected strong testimonials, and ran the team's playbook exactly as designed.

Coachability plus trust in the process compounds results even when initial expectations are low. Pre-qualify for coachability signals, not just revenue size.

**Testimonials as retention mechanism:** Video testimonials serve dual purpose — social proof AND deepened client commitment. One client converted from one-time to ongoing the same day they gave a video testimonial. Collecting testimonials should be treated as a retention tool, not just a marketing asset.

**What clients actually want:** Clients ultimately want to make money. Every service touchpoint should be evaluated through the lens of whether it moves the client closer to measurable financial outcomes — not just engagement or brand metrics. Revenue results are the true north for client satisfaction and retention.

## Closing Burned Prospects

For prospects who've been burned by a previous vendor:

- Offer a 30-day opt-out within a longer contract
- Exact framing: 'If you're not happy in 30 days, just tell me and I'll let you out of the agreement'
- This reduces perceived risk while still allowing enough time to demonstrate value

The three concerns burned prospects consistently raise: (1) trial period flexibility, (2) written deliverables with timeframes, (3) clear communication cadence. Address all three in one conversation.

## Underperforming Campaign Protocol (48-Hour Rule)

- If a campaign is outside KPI for 48 hours, flag immediately and reduce spend
- Never let underperforming campaigns run at full budget while you fix them
- Define a specific measurable win target for the week, not vague improvement goals
  - Wrong: 'improve the VSL'
  - Right: '100 leads this week' or '10 booked appointments by Wednesday'
- If the current funnel isn't delivering, consider launching a parallel funnel type to increase lead volume immediately

## Weekly Accountability Meeting Cadence

- 8:00-10:30 AM: review each client, write personalized update, send to client
- 10:30-11:00 AM: fill stat sheet (last 7 days, last 30 days, launch statuses)
- 11:00 AM Monday meeting: leadership reviews stat sheets in breakout sessions
- Rule: come prepped, never figure things out live during the meeting

## Post-Call Recap Protocol

After every client call, send a summary containing:
- Key decisions made
- Action items (who owns what, by when)
- Next steps / what's being built
- Fathom recording link

Half of client retention is re-selling them on the vision on every call. When a client starts losing faith, re-sell them on what's happening and how it works. Voice notes are especially effective for building personal connection and owning mistakes.
